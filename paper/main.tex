\title{Reading the agent instead of running the simulator: model-internal signals for selective
digital-twin validation of large language model agents in process control}

\author[ic]{Javal Vyas\corref{cor1}}
\ead{j.vyas24@imperial.ac.uk}
\author[ic]{\todo{co-authors}}
\cortext[cor1]{Corresponding author}
\address[ic]{\todo{Department}, Imperial College London, London, United Kingdom}

\begin{abstract}
Large language model (LLM) agents that propose corrective actions for industrial processes are usually guarded
by a digital twin that simulates every proposal before execution. Simulation cost grows with horizon and
fidelity, whereas the agent's forward pass, which already computes token probabilities, attention and hidden
states, costs a fixed amount. We ask whether these model-internal signals can replace part of the simulation. A
calibrated probe on the agent's internals estimates each proposal's failure risk and routes it to execution,
rejection or simulation, with a Clopper--Pearson guarantee on the failure rate of unchecked executions. In a
pre-registered study of five open-weight models and two domains (graph path planning;
fault recovery of a continuous stirred-tank reactor), internals alone, with no plant model, removed 11--79\% of
validator calls in path planning, with certified accepts for four models, and 12--53\% in the reactor, almost
entirely through rejections that were 95--98\% precise. In path planning, internals added to observable features
under a flexible probe, and against observables that do not reuse the validator's logic they added 11--37
points; in the reactor, a flexible surrogate of the plant absorbed their contribution. Attention on the prompt
region that decides the answer explains why: it predicts failures caused by not consulting relevant
information (AUROC 0.72--0.88), but not failures of reasoning over correctly consulted information (0.48--0.72).
In 2000 closed-loop episodes, internals-based probes accepted failing retries, a shift outside the guarantee.
Restricting unchecked execution to first proposals restored safety (0--2 unsafe executions per 400 episodes)
while avoiding 29--65\% of validator calls.
\end{abstract}
